# Rules reference

## Game model and roles

*Oath* is a continuing-history game. A game changes later games; a campaign has no intrinsic end. A bounded competitive sequence is an **era**, whose endpoint is chosen by the group (suggested default: 20 lineage points). Players may join or leave between games (CR p. 5).

Each player controls a lineage/color and one pawn. Roles are:

- **Exile**: independent player; may win as Usurper or Visionary.
- **Chancellor**: head of the Empire and holder of the Grand Scepter.
- **Citizen**: Imperial player allied structurally with the Chancellor but able to raid Imperials, attack Imperial sites, and win as Successor.
- **Imperial**: collective term for Chancellor and Citizens (CR pp. 34-35).

## Core state and terminology

- The map has three regions in order: **Cradle**, **Provinces**, **Hinterland**. **Hinterward** is top-to-bottom Cradle, then Provinces, then Hinterland; **Cradleward** reverses that order (CR pp. 9, 45).
- A pawn's **site** is its current location. A player's board holds favor, secrets, warbands, and Supply state (CR p. 14).
- Six denizen suits exist: Discord, Arcane, Order, Hearth, Beast, and Nomad. A site may not exceed its printed denizen capacity; an adviser area normally holds at most three cards (CR p. 14).
- A site is ruled by exactly one player or by the bandits. Any player warband there establishes rule. After any action, an empty site is immediately filled with bandit warbands equal to capacity (CR p. 15).
- A player has access to cards at their pawn's site, sites they rule, and their play area (relics, banners, advisers, legacies). Power costs can be placed only on empty cards (CR p. 15).
- Relics and banners have no holding limit and can be stolen. Facedown relics have no power, but still count for victory and legacy conditions (CR pp. 15, 25, 27; NF p. 14).

## Setup

### Very first game only

1. Prepare common pieces; start banners on Mob and Wandering Flame with one relevant resource; use Oath of Supremacy; shuffle relics (CR p. 6).
2. Deal eight faceup sites from Recent Sites. Populate starting resources, facedown relics equal to slots, bandits equal to capacity, and a matching ruined edifice at each Homeland (CR p. 6).
3. Build a 60-denizen pool (10 of each suit), seed regional discards and player hands, and build the world deck with Vision packets: 10 denizens + 2 Visions above 15 denizens + 3 Visions above the remaining denizens (CR p. 6).
4. Put 3 favor in each suit bank, +1 per bank at 5+ players, and +1 to a bank per matching edifice on the map. Initialize Round, Usurper Limiter (round 4), Visions Drawn, and relic deck (CR p. 7).
5. Randomize seating/first player. Everyone is an Exile with 1 favor, 1 secret, 3 warbands on board, full-left Supply, and 2 random legacies. In order, place pawn, keep one legacy dormant, keep one of three denizens as adviser, discard the rest to the next regional discard cycle, then reveal any facedown advisers and relics except site-only advisers, without When Played (CR pp. 7, 13).

No one begins Chancellor or Oathkeeper; the first player to satisfy Supremacy takes the title (NF p. 8). Do not reuse first-game setup for a new era unless fully resetting the box (CR p. 7; NF p. 9).

### Later games

1. Initialize map tracks, end die if Imperials play, relic deck, prior Oathkeeper goal/title, and stored world-deck denizens with Visions removed (CR p. 8).
2. Players recover their stored color, legacies, starting adviser, 1 favor, 1 secret, 3 board warbands, and Supply. Imperials use purple warbands. Chancellor takes the Chancellor board, Grand Scepter, and one chosen facedown Reliquary relic (CR p. 8; the pick is Setup's first step, [Empire ruling R29](ambiguities.md#empire-rulings)). Digital: the shared bank shows a non-empty Reliquary facedown, and Golden Key's Imperial holder may always peek at it and, with its ACTION, take a relic from it ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), items 9, 10, 14 and 18). Digital: Circle of Swords' holder starts with 8 board warbands, an Imperial holder's purple ones after every other Imperial board's 3, and Scepter Bearer's Imperial holder takes the Grand Scepter in the Chancellor's place, at game start ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 5 and 6).
3. If a Chancellor exists, restore Imperial sites from Recent Sites until the Empire divider, intact edifices/faceup denizens, stored facedown relics, and one Imperial warband each (CR p. 9). Purple warbands are limited to 24: boards first, then sites Cradlemost first; a site the supply misses takes bandits ([R30](ambiguities.md#empire-rulings)). Remaining sites keep their stored cards and Homelands need no stored edifice ([R26, R27](ambiguities.md#empire-rulings)); the Empire always holds a site ([R28](ambiguities.md#empire-rulings)). Digital: under an Exile's Iron Hand, when the holder's lineage was in the previous game, each of these sites, or each former Empire site with no Chancellor, takes one of the holder's warbands instead, after their board, and the Empire may rule no site ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 7 and 59).
4. With no Chancellor: force Foundation I to Wide Horizons and V to Mob's Favor; restore sites with ruined edifices, stored relics, capacity bandits; clear Reliquary; fill empty relic slots; turn stored Citizens into Exiles; choose first player; use Usurper Limiter and no end die (CR p. 9).
5. Resolve six foundation setup cards in order. Their normal/altered faces determine map filling, banks/resources, player wealth, world composition, and banner side (CR pp. 10-11). Digital: before them, Rival's Exile holder fills the three Cradlemost empty slots with the next Recent Sites by the Empire rules, one of their warbands on each; Foundation II counts their denizens and Imperial Maps fills only the slots left ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), item 8). Digital: World Crafter's holder fills Imperial Maps' slots in the Chancellor's place, choosing the end for each slot, and chooses the end of every Explore under Wide Horizons; Populist's holder may divide the six banks' favor evenly and take the remainder; Matchmaker's holder keeps a legacy for every other player in Quiet Ambitions ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 20 to 23).
6. Populate site resources, then build the world deck from the foundation-determined starting denizen stack, combining Vision packets as instructed (CR p. 12). Digital: Secret Society's holder may first take a Vision but the Conspiracy as a facedown adviser, advancing Visions Drawn, and the other four are shuffled back in the packets' layout; then Seer's holder places every Vision at the position they choose ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 31 and 32). A Secret Society holder over their adviser limit discards the excess right after placing their pawn (step 7; items 31 and 36).
7. In order, players place pawn (subject to Imperial placement; Exiles at the Hintermost Empire site or a bandit site, [R25](ambiguities.md#empire-rulings)), choose adviser, and reveal cards as allowed (CR p. 13). Digital: under Iron Hand, when its holder was in the previous game, every player may place at any site, and Rival's holder also at the sites their warbands hold ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 7 and 8). Quest to Distant Lands' holder may place no pawn; their Choose Adviser and Reveal Cards then wait for their first Wake (items 44 and 46). After their pawn, or after placing none, The Standard Bearer's holder may take a banner from the shared bank with two more of its resource on it, and Wealthy's holder takes 1 favor from each of two other players or 3 from the shared bank (items 42, 43 and 47). A Quest holder with no pawn may then swap one Dispossessed card, which only they see, for a starting adviser or a card of their hand, when they have one to give (items 45 and 57). Right after their pawn, a Secret Society holder over their adviser limit discards the excess; a holder with no pawn does so at their first Wake (items 36 and 44). At Reveal Cards an Exile may reveal the Vision Secret Society gave them onto their Vision space (item 58).

## Round and turn flow

A round gives each player one turn, clockwise from Chancellor; without a Chancellor, start from the first-player marker. Each turn resolves **Wake -> Act -> Rest** (CR p. 17).

### Wake

1. If an Exile holds Oathkeeper, flip it to Usurper unless limited; if already Usurper, they win.
2. If an Exile has a revealed Vision and currently meets it, they win.
3. Use any Wake powers once each.
4. Built-in **Take Wealth**: take one favor or secret from the pawn's site if no enemy pawn is present (CR p. 17). Digital: Light Fingers' holder takes it even beside an enemy pawn ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), item 22).

Digital: a seated player with no pawn, who stayed off the map with Quest to Distant Lands, first places their pawn at any site in play, then chooses an adviser, discards Secret Society's excess advisers, and reveals cards, before step 1 ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), items 36, 44, 46, 52 and 58).

### Act

Take actions in any order, repeated or omitted. Major actions cost Supply; minor actions cost 0 by default (CR p. 17).

### Rest

1. Use each desired Rest power once.
2. Return favor from denizens to matching suit banks. Return secrets from denizens/relics to the player's board. Flip the player's facedown secrets faceup.
3. Refresh Supply to the space corresponding to warbands remaining in the off-board warband bank, then one extra step left per Supply left unspent this turn (CR p. 18).

## Major actions

### Search - 2 to 4 Supply

Choose world deck or current-region discard. Regional discard costs 2; world cost is the Visions Drawn track value. Draw one at a time, normally three; stop on a Vision drawn from world and advance the track. Keep one and discard the rest to the next-region pile (Cradle -> Provinces -> Hinterland -> Cradle). Play the kept card to the site or advisers subject to icons/capacity, or discard it. Playing a denizen to a site gains one matching-suit favor (CR p. 20).

### Travel - 1 to 4 Supply before modifiers

Choose a destination and pay the region matrix:

| From | Cradle | Provinces | Hinterland |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cradle | 1 (other Cradle) | 2 | 4 |
| Provinces | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Hinterland | 4 | 2 | 3 (other Hinterland) |

Then move the pawn. Site powers such as Coast, Island, Mountain, and Pass modify or restrict this (CR p. 21). Digital: an Imperial holding Ancestral Lands spends at most 1 Supply to reach the Cradle, and Pathfinder's holder ignores the Mountain, Island (Lost Lagoon's included) and Pass travel powers ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), items 19, 20 and 25).

### Campaign - 2 Supply

Choose exactly one type (CR pp. 22-23):

- **Conquest**: defender is the ruler of the attacker's site. Must target that site; may add other sites ruled by the same defender.
- **Raid**: defender is an enemy pawn at the attacker's site. Must target the pawn; may also target any number (including zero) of that player's faceup relics and banners.

Sequence: choose targets; form attacker dice up to board warband force and defender dice from targets (raid pawn adds 2); attacker then defender use battle plans; attacker rolls and counts swords while suffering skull losses; attacker may sacrifice force warbands for +1 attack each; defender rolls shields/doublers and adds target-force warbands; attacker wins only if attack strictly exceeds defense; defeated player loses half its force rounded down and returns the rest to board (bandits lose all targeted forces); resolve conquest placement or raid theft/discards/burn/move-pawn; resolve remaining end/victory/defeat battle plans.

On successful raid: take targeted faceup relics/banners; discard defender's facedown advisers and facedown relics (removed until Chronicle), burn half defender favor rounded down, and relocate defender pawn to another site (CR p. 23).

### Muster - 1 Supply

Place one favor on an empty denizen at the pawn's site. Gain one warband to board plus one for each faceup adviser matching that denizen's suit (CR p. 24).

### Trade - 1 Supply

Use an empty denizen at the pawn's site. For favor, place one secret and gain one favor plus one per matching faceup adviser from the matching suit bank. For secrets, place one favor and burn one favor, then gain one secret per matching faceup adviser (including zero) (CR p. 24; NF p. 13). Digital: Beloved's holder may also trade on the faceup advisers of other players whose pawns are at their site ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), item 23).

### Forge - 1 Supply

Requires ruling the pawn's site, a Forge icon, and three empty denizens. Place the site's printed favor/secrets onto those three denizens, one resource per denizen in any order, then draw a facedown relic (CR p. 25).

### Recover - 1+ Supply

Roll two defense dice; repeatedly pay 1 Supply for two more dice. If accumulated shields, including doublers, meet/exceed the site's Recover Difficulty, peek at site relics and take one facedown (CR p. 25).

### Challenge - 1 Supply

Choose a banner not held by the actor. If enemy-held, pawns must share a site. Challenger must have more of the relevant resource than is on the banner. Resolve the banner's left-ribbon removal/distribution, take it, then place more relevant resources on it than it held before the challenge (CR p. 26).

## Minor actions

Default cost is 0 Supply. Core minor actions include:

- play/discard a facedown adviser as if just searched;
- use an Action power; Digital: a seated player's active legacy is a source of its ACTION, named in the Act menu, its burnt cost paid as a card's ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), items 1 to 6);
- reveal/peek at relics held by the player;
- move warbands between board and a ruled site at the pawn;
- negotiate with a co-located player, exchanging favor and relics (not secrets/banners), with mutual consent; Digital: High Priest's holder may give secrets, one way ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), items 24 and 28);
- banner-specific resource placement;
- self-exile under its cost/conditions;
- applicable Imperial warband transfers (CR p. 27; CR pp. 32, 34-35).

## Victory and game end

### Oathkeeper and Usurper

The current Oathkeeper goal is one of: Supremacy (rule more sites than every other player, at least one), Protection (hold most relics, at least one), The People (People's Favor), or Devotion (Darkest Secret). Title changes immediately after an action changes qualification; current holder retains ties, otherwise holder chooses among tied qualifiers. Bandits do not compete (CR p. 16).

An Exile who starts a turn holding Oathkeeper flips it to Usurper; if they start their next turn still Usurper, they win. With no Empire, flipping is barred until round 4 (CR pp. 16, 19).

### Visions

An Exile may reveal one true Vision. At the start of their turn they win if its goal is met and at least three Visions have been drawn from the world deck. The four true Visions mirror the four Oathkeeper goals. Conspiracy is not a victory Vision (CR pp. 16, 30).

### Empire endings

At ends of rounds 5/6/7, if Chancellor or Citizen is Oathkeeper, roll the end die; Chancellor wins on 6, then 5-6, then 3-6 respectively. At round 8 resolve War Exhaustion: qualifying Imperial Oathkeeper -> Chancellor; Exile Usurper; qualifying revealed Vision (tie order Conquest, Rebellion, Sanctuary, Faith); otherwise Chancellor (CR p. 19).

If Chancellor would win, a Citizen satisfying the current Successor goal wins instead. Successor mappings are: Supremacy -> more relics than Chancellor and every other Citizen; The People -> Darkest Secret; Protection -> People's Favor; Devotion -> Grand Scepter (CR p. 19).

Without Empire, round 8 uses War Exhaustion while skipping Stable Regime and Chancellor steps; if still no winner, Oathkeeper wins, otherwise random (CR p. 19).

## Imperial laws

- **Seal of the Empire.** On an Imperial player's turn, that player rules the Imperial sites (CR p. 35).
- **Steward of Imperial Domains.** Outside such a turn, the Chancellor rules the Imperial sites (CR p. 35).
- **Imperial warbands.** Imperial players share one bank of 24 purple warbands, and a killed purple warband returns to it (CR p. 35).
- **Refreshing Supply.** The Chancellor refreshes by the warbands in the purple bank; a Citizen matches the Chancellor's current Supply and keeps their unspent Supply, capped at 7 (CR p. 35).
- **Chancellor's Eye.** A Citizen asks the Chancellor's consent to move warbands from their site to their board (CR p. 35).
- **Warband Transfers.** An Imperial player may move warbands to or from another Imperial player's board when that player's pawn is at their site and that player consents (CR p. 35).
- **Imperial Infighting.** An Imperial player may raid another Imperial player, and a Citizen may conquer Imperial sites, which the Chancellor defends (CR p. 35). Digital: Keeper of Order is an Imperial's battle plan against an Imperial, ±2 attack dice, the attacker's skulls ignored, lent through Imperial Tactics ([Legacy in-play powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-in-play-powers-rulings), items 5, 21 and 26).
- **Imperial Tactics.** In a Campaign, an Imperial player may use battle plans in another Imperial player's play area with their consent, and plans at Imperial sites with the Chancellor's consent; any Imperial player may pay a plan's costs or meet its requirements, even split between players. The Oathkeeper title is a defensive battle plan. The attacker and defender cannot use the same plan (CR p. 35).

## Role changes

- **Becoming a Citizen.** The player's board turns to its Citizen side; their warbands on the board and the map become purple, the player choosing which when the purple bank runs short; their revealed Vision is discarded; a Usurper title turns to its Oathkeeper side, and under Supremacy passes to the Chancellor; their Act phase ends if it is their turn; their Supply refreshes to 7 (CR p. 34).
- **Becoming the Chancellor.** In a game with no Empire, a "become a Citizen" power makes the player the Chancellor instead: they also take the Chancellor board, the purple bank and the Grand Scepter (CR p. 34). The Usurper Limiter is removed at once (ruling R5), and the next round starts with them (ruling R4).
- **Exile.** A Citizen's board turns to its Exile side; the purple warbands on it become warbands of their colour, the excess returning to the purple bank, while the Imperial warbands on the map stay; their Act phase ends if it is their turn; their Supply refreshes to 7 (CR p. 34).
- **Self-exile.** As a minor action, a Citizen gives the Chancellor 1 favor per Imperial warband on their board, at least 3, and is exiled (CR p. 34).
- **The Grand Scepter's Action.** Its Exile holder may become a Citizen (card OATH-GrandScepter-NF).
- **The Grand Scepter in negotiation.** Its holder may offer Citizenship to Exiles among the negotiators, never to themself; accepting the deal is the Exile's consent (CR p. 34).
- **The Grand Scepter's battle plan.** For 3 burnt favor, an Imperial player victorious against a Citizen may exile them (CR p. 34, ruling R23).
- **"Become a Citizen" denizens.** Martial Culture (defeating another Exile), Long-Lost Heir, Bewitch (the most secrets), Ancient Pact (the Darkest Secret) and Royal Ambitions (the most sites); the last three also take a random relic from the Reliquary (rulings R21-R23).
- **First game.** A group's first game is advised to ignore "become a Citizen" powers (CR p. 34, ambiguity F8); a setup option does so, which game start turns on for a first game only: a brand-new Chronicle with no Empire and no history ([Foundations rulings](ambiguities.md#foundations-rulings), item 17). One with history is a first game played exile-only, which starts with every stored legacy wiped back into the deck ([Legacy Setup powers rulings](ambiguities.md#legacy-setup-powers-rulings), item 60).

## Rules precedence and timing

- Card powers may add actions and trigger effects while using glossary-defined terms (CR p. 42).
- "Cannot" overrides "can"; "must" is mandatory; "may" is optional. If multiple "cannot" rules conflict, use the more specific one (CR p. 42).
- Component supply is normally limited; dice are unlimited. If a limited component runs out, ignore additional placement (CR p. 42).
- Public and private information are enumerated on CR p. 42. A player may choose/share all their private information.
- Consent must be actively enforced when an "Enemies cannot" persistent effect applies; forgetting waives it for that moment (CR p. 43).
- A persistent power that restricts its ruler's enemies may be ignored with that ruler's consent, except inside a Campaign action unless the power's own text grants it (CR p. 29). The bandits never consent.
- New Foundations makes "Enemies cannot" powers optional for their ruler, decided at the moment of use (NF p. 16). The digital game always enforces them and asks the ruler explicitly ([consent design](../superpowers/specs/2026-10/2026-10-08-consent-system-design.md)).
