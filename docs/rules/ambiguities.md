# Ambiguities and implementation questions

These are not claimed errors in the rulebook. They are places where an implementation needs component data, a product decision, or a more explicit formalization.

## Component-data dependencies

1. **Card powers and exact costs.** The catalog contains stable printed IDs, reviewed text, and handler keys. Most denizens and relics now have executable Scala behavior; the cards still without it are kept in `src/main/scala/distantlands/catalog/holding/`: 26 denizens, 10 relics, 11 sites, 22 edifices, and all 36 legacies. Many actions can be modified or replaced by these powers (CR pp. 28-30, 42). Each legacy has its printed goal, suit and altered foundation ([Legacies design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-13-legacies-design.md), slice 1); their powers are still in holding.
2. **Foundation faces.** The catalog contains the six normal/altered records, and game start reads the faces a Chronicle stores; a first game's are all normal ([Foundations rulings](#foundations-rulings), item 17). The altered faces of Foundations II (Movers and Shakers) and IV (Teeming World) are built at setup, and so is Wide Horizons (I), which leaves the map's remaining slots empty for its Explore action to fill in play. An altered V, Grand Council, starts the People's Favor on its Grand Council face, and an altered VI, Festival, the Darkest Secret on its Festival face ([Foundations design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-11-foundations-design.md), slices 5 and 6). The Beacon's Shape Foundation restores one altered face in the Chronicle. Foundation III deals legacies at game start: Quiet Ambitions draws 2 for each player to keep one in Setup, and Public Ambitions deals a faceup row of 4 ([Legacies design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-13-legacies-design.md), slice 2). The [Foundations rulings](#foundations-rulings) hold the batch's rulings and readings, and the [traceability table](implementation-traceability.md#setup) lists the evidence.
3. **Player-board tables/icons.** Supply refresh thresholds and some costs are
   graphically encoded on player boards. Trade yield is resolved by the current
   combined rulebook prose: favor is 1 + matches; secrets equal matches (CR
   pp. 18, 24).
4. **Banner ribbons and non-default faces.** CR p. 32 explains ribbon semantics, but machine execution needs exact component-side icon sequences and initial values.
5. **Component behavior coverage.** Printed identities and the runtime catalog are complete, but legacy powers, banner ribbons, and the other cards still in the holding package (item 1) still require typed executable handlers.

## Formalization decisions

1. **Action boundary.** Several automatic checks happen "after any action" (bandit refill, Oathkeeper transfer), while powers can introduce actions and nested effects. Define a transaction boundary and when state-based checks reach a fixed point (CR pp. 15-16, 42).
2. **Simultaneous effects.** Turn player chooses order during a turn; current Chancellor chooses during Chronicle. Define who chooses outside both contexts and whether ordering choices are logged (CR p. 42).
3. **Consent timeout/forgetting.** "Enemies cannot" effects fail if not enforced at the moment. A digital implementation needs an explicit prompt/auto-consent policy so silence is deterministic (CR p. 43).
   Resolved for the digital game: the engine always enforces, and a blocked player asks the ruler explicitly; a ruler may set a standing per-player answer ([consent design](../superpowers/specs/2026-10/2026-10-08-consent-system-design.md), rulings 3 and 10).
   An official ruling on the Hall of Ministers makes enforcement all-or-nothing: once waived, it lets through every discard that happens at the same time. The engine treats one operation batch as that moment (consent design, ruling 3).
4. **Private information.** The rules permit owners to view/share their private data. Multiplayer UI needs visibility and reveal/audit policies for facedown advisers, relics, drawn cards, and secret selections (CR p. 42).
5. **Unlimited dice vs limited pieces.** Dice are unlimited; other components are limited and extra placement is ignored. Decide whether digital inventory should preserve physical limits exactly (CR p. 42).
6. **Random winner/coin flips.** No-Empire exhaustion can choose randomly, and unclear Tribunal promise breach uses a coin flip. Specify RNG seed and event log behavior (CR pp. 19, 30).
7. **Ties by role/context (resolved only for fixed first-game Supremacy).**
   Supremacy's ordinary qualification is strict: rule more sites than every
   other player, so an initially unheld title is not assigned across a highest
   tie. CR p. 16 then supplies title-specific exceptions: a current holder in
   the highest tie retains it; if the holder is displaced by multiple tied
   leaders, that holder chooses the recipient. The current bounded evaluator
   implements all three cases; the last creates a durable choice visible and
   actionable only by the displaced holder. This is not a generic tie
   resolver; legacy, Vision exhaustion, era, and other ties remain unresolved
   in their own contexts (CR pp. 16, 19, 36-37).
8. **First game exception.** CR recommends ignoring "become Citizen" powers in the first game, wording this as a recommendation rather than a strict prohibition. Product rules need a configurable or explicit interpretation (CR p. 30; CR p. 34 uses similar guidance).
    Resolved in part: a setup option ignores these powers and shows them disabled (Empire slice 3a). Game start turns it on for a first game only, as the rulebook recommends it for "your very first game" (owner's ruling, 2026-11-12); the Settings Feature phase lets players change it.
9. **Campaign target legality under Pass and zero-capacity sites.** Target generation must combine campaign type, ruler, region Pass consent, and special no-capacity cases (CR pp. 21-22, 30-31).
10. **Chronicle actor availability.** Banner task assignment can target "anyone"; player attendance may change between games. Define whether absent/stored lineages can be assigned tasks or whether "anyone" means current participants (CR p. 36).
    Resolved: only players seated for the game receive tasks ([Chronicle rulings](#chronicle-rulings), ruling 1).

## Empire rulings

Rulings for the Empire in play ([Empire phase design](../superpowers/specs/2026-10/2026-10-29-empire-phase-design.md), "Rulings"). Slice 1a implements R1 to R3 and the Usurper ruling; slice 3a implements R4, R5 and the Usurper flip of R7.

R1. **One ruler.** On an Imperial player's turn only that player rules Imperial sites; the Chancellor does not also rule them. A site has exactly one ruler (CR p. 15).

R2. **The Chancellor outside any turn.** Outside any turn, as in the end-of-round checks and the Chronicle, the Chancellor rules Imperial sites.

R3. **A Citizen's Supply.** A Citizen refreshes to the Chancellor's current Supply, which includes the Chancellor's own carried surplus, plus their own unspent Supply, capped at 7.

R4. **A mid-round Chancellor.** A Chancellor who appears mid-round leaves the current round's order unchanged; the next round starts with them. The round's first player is recorded when the round begins (CR p. 17; Empire slice 3a).

R5. **The Usurper Limiter.** Its limit applies only in games with no Empire, so it ends, and its marker is removed, when an Empire starts in play (CR p. 19; Empire slice 3a).

R7. **No Imperial Usurper in play.** An Imperial player never holds the Usurper side of the Oathkeeper title during play. An Exile who holds it and becomes a Citizen flips it to Oathkeeper at once (Empire slice 3a implements the flip), so domain validation rejects an Imperial player on the Usurper side outside the Chronicle phase. The check is suspended in the Chronicle phase, where the Chronicle crowns the Usurper winner after the game has ended.

R8. **Warband Transfers consent names the transfer.** The partner is asked after the player has chosen the way and the amount, so the ask names them: "Asked {Blue} for consent to give them 3 warbands." Each consent covers one exact transfer. A refusal stops only that way and amount for the turn; the player may ask about a different one.

R9. **Military Parade counts one enemy.** The New Foundations card applies to the one enemy only. Its "including Imperial Allies" text is a base-game concept and is ignored.

R10. **Plans at Imperial sites.** In a Campaign, plans at Imperial sites belong to the Chancellor's side on every turn. The Chancellor uses them freely; any other Imperial needs the Chancellor's consent, even on their own turn (Empire slice 1b-ii).

R11. **Split costs.** Every cost of a battle plan may be split between Imperial players, including a variable amount. The warband sacrifice still comes from the defender's force (Empire slice 1b-ii).

R12. **Shared requirements.** Any Imperial player who meets a plan's requirement (holding the Darkest Secret, holding the People's Favor, having their pawn at a site) may meet it for the plan's user, with that player's consent. A requirement met this way for a plan that checks it at the end of the Campaign (Hearts and Minds) stays met until then (CR p. 35; Empire slice 1b-ii).

R13. **Captains and Wild Allies at an Empire site.** A Citizen may use Captains or Wild Allies to campaign from a site the Empire holds, against the Chancellor. The site's warbands join the attack and count once, for the attacker, never in the defense; they do not die in the victory, and only the board's survivors are placed. This follows the Captains FAQ in the BGS card library; Wild Allies has no FAQ but the same wording. The FAQ's "you do not need to target the site" refers to a base-game rule and does not apply in New Foundations.

R14. **Keep.** Keep's site is used by whoever defends it in the Campaign, so a defeated Chancellor keeps Keep at an Empire site even on a Citizen's turn.

R15. **Hospital.** Hospital saves warbands only if its user rules its site by the turn's ruler at the Campaign's end, as its FAQ in the BGS card library says ("if you rule it at the end"). A Chancellor defending on a Citizen's turn may choose it at an Empire site (R10), but it saves nothing; a Citizen using it on their own turn has theirs saved.

R16. **What aid covers.** Imperial Tactics lets an ally pay a plan's costs and meet its requirements, and nothing more. A plan that counts what its user rules or holds (Great Crusade, Pledge of Defense, Kindred Warriors, Nature Worship) counts only the user's own cards. An ally's pawn at a site meets "your pawn is at this site" (Towering Rampart) (CR p. 35; Empire slice 1b-ii).

R17. **The Grand Scepter counts.** Under Supremacy, the Successor holds more relics than each other Imperial player (card OATH-265). The Grand Scepter is a relic, so it counts in its holder's total (CR p. 19; Empire slice 2).

R18. **Short purple bank.** When the purple bank holds fewer warbands than a player becoming a Citizen has on their board and at sites, the player splits the bank's warbands over their board and each site that holds their warbands. At each place, the warbands left over return to the player's own bank, so a site can end with fewer warbands, or none (CR p. 34; Empire slice 3a).

R19. **Supremacy title.** Under the Oath of Supremacy, a player who becomes a Citizen while holding the Oathkeeper title passes it to the Chancellor, on either side: Citizens never hold that title (CR p. 35). A player who becomes the Chancellor keeps it, turned to its Oathkeeper side (CR p. 34; Empire slice 3a).

R20. **No one performs a role change.** Becoming a Citizen, from any source, is performed by no player, so a restriction against a player's placements does not stop its warband replacements. Vow of Wandering refuses only a replacement its holder performs (Bandit Prince, Ballot Box). Chronicle steps have no actor (Chronicle ruling 7), so the Vow restricts nothing in the Chronicle phase (Empire slice 3b).

R21. **"The most."** "Have the most secrets" (Bewitch) and "rule the most sites" (Royal Ambitions) mean strictly more than every other seated player: a tie is not the most. Secrets are counted as the player's area shows them, credits for secrets on cards in play going to the active player; sites are the sites the player rules (Empire slice 3b).

R22. **Empty Reliquary.** Bewitch, Ancient Pact and Royal Ambitions still make the player a Citizen when the Reliquary is empty; only the relic is missed. All three take the relic, as their card text says, facedown (Empire slice 3b).

R23. **Either side.** Martial Culture works for an Exile who defeats another Exile player as attacker or defender, and the Grand Scepter's battle plan for an Imperial player who defeats a Citizen as attacker or defender. Its card database wording is followed: "you may exile them", for 3 burnt favor. A defender who becomes a Citizen or is exiled off-turn ends no Act phase (Empire slice 3b).

R24. **Queued actions after a role change (provisional).** Actions a player has queued, such as Second Wind's Travel and Campaign, still run after a role change ends that player's Act phase (Empire slice 3b). The Martial Culture FAQ suggests Second Wind's second Campaign does not happen once its holder has become a Citizen; revisit this.

R25. **Exile pawn sites.** With an Empire, an Exile places their pawn at the Hintermost Empire site or at a site the bandits rule. A site with no warbands, such as Sunken Isles with capacity 0, is not ruled by bandits, so an Exile may not start there (Empire slice 4).

R26. **Homelands in later games.** The check that each Homeland in play has a stored edifice of its suit applies only to a first game ([Foundations rulings](#foundations-rulings), item 17). Later-game setup places only the edifices stored with each site (CR p. 9, Foundation I; Empire slice 4).

R27. **Stored cards at the remaining sites.** A site filled from the atlas in a later game keeps its stored cards: its edifice on the ruined side, its denizens faceup, its relics facedown. Its empty relic slots, its relic slots less its stored relics and never fewer than 0, are filled from the relic deck. Under Imperial Maps the Chancellor chooses at Setup, right after the Reliquary pick, whether every empty slot is filled from the Recent or the Forgotten end; the sites are placed one at a time going Hinterward, each adding Foundation II's favor for its denizens (Empire slice 4; [Foundations design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-11-foundations-design.md), slice 4).

R28. **The Empire always holds a site.** The Throne's Shape Empire step gives a winner who rules no site the site their pawn stands on, so a Chronicle with a Chancellor and an empty Empire is malformed. Game start refuses it, and an Empire of more than 8 sites (Empire slice 4).

R29. **The Chancellor's Reliquary pick.** The pick is the first Setup step. During Setup the Chancellor alone sees the Reliquary's relics, and keeps one facedown. An empty Reliquary skips the step. The log reads "p1 took a relic from the Reliquary" (Empire slice 4).

R30. **A short purple supply at setup.** Purple warbands are limited to the 24 components, in setup and in play. Setup first gives each Imperial player their 3 board warbands, then places 1 Imperial warband on each Empire site, Cradlemost first, until the supply runs out. An Empire site left without one is otherwise set up as an Empire site: intact edifice, stored relics only, no relic slots filled. The empty-site check the walker runs after each action then fills every empty site with bandits up to its capacity; a capacity-0 site stays empty. Chronicle rulings 20 and 22 keep their unlimited supply, since the Chronicle phase is not play (Empire slice 4).

R31. **Obsidian Cage.** Either side may use the Cage's battle plan. Bandit warbands are always killed, so none reach it. Warbands the enemy's Hospital saves still survive in the force, so they go onto the Cage, not to Hospital's site. The Cage's Action may return caged Imperial warbands to any Imperial board: the Chancellor's or a Citizen's (user, 2026-11-03; [walker follow-ups audit](../testing/audits/walker-follow-ups-audit.md#obsidian-cage-rulings-made-while-implementing)).

## Chronicle rulings

Rulings given by the project owner for the Chronicle Phase ([chronicle.md](chronicle.md)). The rev1 task cards are slightly older than the CR; where they disagree, the CR governs.

1. **Task recipients.** Only players who were seated for the game can receive a Chronicle task (CR p. 36).
2. **Unheld banners.** "If no one holds a banner" is two rules: if no one holds the People's Favor, the winner chooses who takes the World task; if no one holds the Darkest Secret, the winner chooses who takes the Beacon task (CR p. 36).
3. **One player holds World and Beacon.** That player chooses which task to give away and to whom (CR p. 36).
4. **Legacy goal ties.** The CR's friendly (Imperial-only) and unfriendly (any Exile) tie rules govern. The Sun card's "The Chancellor or first player breaks goal ties" is outdated (CR p. 36).
5. **Citizenship.** Only Exiles can be offered Citizenship, including players who became Exiles earlier in the same Chronicle when an Exile won (CR pp. 36-37). The Throne card's "any players" is outdated.
6. **Shape Empire exceptions.** Intact edifices and cards at Enduring sites are not discarded, as the CR says; the Throne card omits both. Some legacies also protect denizens; those arrive with legacy powers (CR p. 37).
7. **Actors.** Most Chronicle steps have no actor and run procedurally. The Stars task has no actor-bound step except Write in the Stars, which the digital version does not implement (CR pp. 40-41).
8. **Play again or store.** The digital phase always ends by journaling the evolved Chronicle; the CR's choice between playing again and storing does not apply (CR p. 41).
9. **Edifice order.** Edifices discarded during play (for example a ruined edifice discarded by a homeland power) or in Shape Empire must not return in Build Edifice while another edifice of their suit is available. The end of Build Edifice shuffles the whole edifice deck, so no order survives between games. The digital version keeps an ordered set-aside edifice list on the live state, empty at game start. Build Edifice offers a random edifice of each suit from the edifices not set aside, and falls back to the first set-aside edifice of that suit only when none is left. The list is not part of the Chronicle (CR p. 37).
10. **Grand Scepter with no Chancellor.** When no one holds the Grand Scepter (a first game leaves it out of the relic pool), the winner takes it from the box into their relics, faceup, during Crown Chancellor. If a player holds it, it moves from that player (CR p. 36).
11. **The Chancellor's warbands in the Throne task.** After Crown Chancellor, "your warbands" in Shape Empire are the Imperial warbands, "sites you rule" are the sites the Empire rules, and "the last warband of yours on the map" is the last Imperial warband. If the Empire rules no sites, the fallback places one Imperial warband at the winner's pawn site (CR p. 37).
12. **Built edifice face.** An edifice placed by Build Edifice is placed intact (CR p. 37).
13. **Lineage point gifts.** The winner may give lineage points only to players who were seated for the game, as for task recipients in ruling 1 (CR p. 37).
14. **Shape Empire discard pile.** A denizen discarded in Shape Empire follows the general discard rule: it goes to the discard pile of the region after its site's region. The World task's Gather Denizens shuffles every discard pile together next, so the pile matters only to the Game Log (CR pp. 37-38).
15. **Newcomers from a short suit.** If a suit the Beacon task holder chose still has too few cards after all older Dispossessed return to their suit sections, the holder draws what remains of that suit. The project owner notes this should not happen in play (CR p. 39).
16. **Locks in the Chronicle.** Lock icons restrict play, not the Chronicle Phase. Gather Denizens takes every adviser, locked or not. Rules that name a protected card, such as Shape Empire's intact edifices, still protect it (CR pp. 37-38).
17. **Rival sites keep every card.** Archive Rival Sites stores every card left on a rival site with it, not only relics and intact edifices. After Shape Empire, a rival Enduring site can still hold denizens or a ruined edifice; they are stored too (CR p. 40).
18. **The Stars on the table.** The Stars steps change nothing on the table. The Chronicle stores no pieces, so Clean Up's piece returns change nothing it records. The digital version reads the finished table once, at the end of the Chronicle Phase, and stores the sites as Archive Rival Sites, Clean Up and Store order them (CR pp. 40-41).
19. **The scorepad's roles.** The era scorepad records each player's role when Score Lineage Points records their score, after the Sun has crowned, so a winning Exile is recorded as Chancellor ([chronicle.md](chronicle.md), the Throne, step 6: "circle the Chancellor's score").

20. **A broken Empire's sites.** When an Exile wins, each site that held Imperial warbands takes one warband of the former Chancellor's colour once the Imperial warbands are removed, so the former Chancellor rules it as an Exile through the rest of the Chronicle. This deviates from CR p. 36, which leaves those sites empty and has the Throne place an Imperial warband on each site with no warbands if the former Chancellor accepts Citizenship. A former Chancellor who declines keeps the sites. Their bank holds 14 less their board warbands, which turn to their colour as they are exiled; when it runs short, the step assumes an unlimited supply and still places one warband on each of those sites, as the Throne does (ruling 22). A boxed former Chancellor places none, and those sites stay empty (Empire slice 3b).

21. **Offer Citizenship.** The Throne holder offers Citizenship to any seated Exiles, none included; boxed lineages are never offered. The offered Exiles answer at once, in any order, Decline first; each answer is public and applies when it is given (user, 2026-11-02; [simultaneous decisions design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-02-simultaneous-decisions-design.md)). An Exile who accepts becomes a Citizen, returns their board warbands, and keeps one Imperial warband at each site they rule. No other Citizenship step, and not the first-game option, applies in the Chronicle (CR p. 37; Empire slice 3b).

22. **Unlimited purple at the Throne.** Offer Citizenship assumes an unlimited purple supply, since it marks sites as the Empire's; purple warbands the bank lacks are added to it (CR p. 37; Empire slice 3b).

23. **Caged warbands at the Throne.** When the Throne returns the relics to the Reliquary, every warband on a card (Obsidian Cage) returns to its supply, since the game has ended; no board takes them (user, 2026-11-03).

## Foundations rulings

Rulings and readings for the six foundation setup cards ([Foundations design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-11-foundations-design.md)). Items 1 to 8 were taken with the project owner on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11; items 1 to 5 are the Foundations design's rulings 1 to 5. Items 9 to 15 are the readings each slice's plan took, the most literal reading of the New Foundations text; the owner approved them on 2026-11-12, and in a second review that day decided the ones an item marks as the owner's.

1. **Foundation III waits for Legacies.** Both faces are accepted at game start and do nothing. The Beacon may still restore an altered III (Foundations design, ruling 1). Answered by the Legacies design, slice 2: both faces now deal ([Legacies rulings](#legacies-rulings)).
2. **Explore borrows only Recover's dice.** No power that names Recover or recovering applies to Explore: Silver Charm, Relic Patrols and Drowned Library stay out, though Silver Charm's text names only spending Supply to roll defense dice. Jinx ("after you roll [attack-die] or [defense-die] for any reason") applies, through the generic reroll window (Foundations design, ruling 2).
3. **No Empire sets the Reliquary aside.** "Discard all relics from the Reliquary. They return in the Chronicle Phase." The Reliquary's relics stay in the campaign all game and never enter play; the slot fill cannot draw them. Crown Chancellor discards them whoever wins, as an Exile's win does, and Stock Reliquary never adds to them (owner, 2026-11-12; Foundations design, ruling 3).
4. **Explore's line** is "Explored Forgotten Site: Found Deep Woods", or "Explored Recent Site: Found ..." for the Recent end (Foundations design, ruling 4).
5. **The shared bank has no count.** Taking favor from it changes nothing but the taker's board (Foundations design, ruling 5).
6. **Explore, the Festival and a game with no Empire.** Any role may Explore. The Festival's marker is placed automatically at each of its holder's Rests, with no question, since the face says "must". With no Empire, the Oathkeeper title and both banners start as in a first game. Under Wide Horizons the Act menu shows Explore disabled with its reason while it cannot be taken; under Imperial Maps no one can ever take it, since Foundation I never changes during a game, so the menu leaves it out, as it leaves out an option the player's role can never use (slice 3b review).
7. **A banner moved to the shared bank.** Hall of Mockery and Lost Guardians "move it to the shared bank": a held banner goes back to the shared bank, when the site is explored and at Setup alike. A Chancellor who starts with the People's Favor or the Darkest Secret loses it at Setup's end when the matching ruined edifice is on the map (slice 3b).
8. **Imperial Maps' question.** The Recent option names the Atlas's first site, which the rulebook makes public: "Recent Sites (Buried Giant first)". The Forgotten end's site is not named, and nothing else about the Atlas is shown before the fill. This closes the deferral [R27](#empire-rulings) recorded (slice 4).
9. **Movers and Shakers and Teeming World** (slice 2). Under Movers and Shakers every board starts with 6 favor, the Chancellor's included. Under Teeming World a lineage that stores three or more advisers draws none and keeps them all. Players draw in seat order, and a lineage's stored advisers come before the ones it draws. A site-only denizen drawn is kept facedown and cannot be revealed in Setup. Choose Adviser is skipped, and Reveal Cards still runs. An uneven rest leaves the Cradle pile, then the Provinces pile, one card more, and the first card dealt to a pile is its top (decided in the slice 2 review). A Search of the Visions-only deck draws one Vision and stops; once the five are drawn the world deck is empty and cannot be searched, unless an Oaths of Loyalty holder buried cards below the Visions ([Legacy Chronicle powers rulings](#legacy-chronicle-powers-rulings), items 32 and 38). The Teeming World readings are the owner's (2026-11-12).
10. **Setup With No Empire** (slice 3a). The absent Chancellor's lineage becomes an Exile in the box with the Citizens. A former Empire with no site is refused, as an Empire's is ([R28](#empire-rulings)). A Foundation I already on Wide Horizons keeps its sources. Relic slots are filled Cradlemost first while the relic deck lasts, and the rest stay empty. If no site holds bandits, an Exile may place their pawn at any site. A power that takes a Reliquary relic finds a set-aside Reliquary empty. On a map with empty slots Shattered Portal's wrap skips them, and a roll that is a multiple of the number of sites leaves the pawn where it stands. The walker refuses a required choice that declares no option, so such an action is shown unavailable. The owner decided the refused siteless former Empire, the kept sources, the Cradlemost relic fill, the any-site pawn fallback, Shattered Portal and the walker's refusal (2026-11-12). Powers that make a player a Citizen apply, as in every game but the first ([F8](#formalization-decisions)).
11. **Explore** (slice 3b). Only a power that hooks one of Explore's own windows changes an Explore, and none does; Brass Army still asks its holder as their pawn is placed. The next slot is the first empty slot going Hinterward. Who rules the Hintermost site does not matter. The explorer chooses Recent or Forgotten after the roll succeeds, without seeing either site, and a later Explore may take the other end; one site left is not asked about and is logged as a Recent Site. The site is populated as Foundation I prints while the relic deck and the bandit bank last, and its denizens add no favor to the suit banks. WHEN EXPLORED resolves after the pawn is placed, and no ruler is asked to consent. Broken Clock advances the round marker at once and does nothing on round 8. An Explore, stopped or not, is an action. Lost Doors and Ruined Temple wait for the cards that act on legacies (a ROADMAP item).
12. **Imperial Maps** (slice 4). An Atlas too short to fill every slot is refused at game start. A Citizen standing in for an absent Chancellor answers. A relic deck that runs short leaves the last relic slots open. Foundation II's favor counts a placed site's ruined edifice and is added as each site is placed. One answer fills every slot, the Forgotten end placing the Atlas's last site in the Cradlemost empty slot. With one site in the Atlas nothing is asked and the line says Recent; an Empire of eight sites asks nothing. The owner ruled that case never an issue, as the Atlas holds far more than eight sites; the code keeps handling it. A placed site's SETUP half resolves at Setup's end. Setup has no turn, so every Setup line names its player. The owner decided the refused short Atlas, the open relic slots, the per-denizen favor counting ruined edifices, the SETUP timing and the named player (2026-11-12).
13. **Grand Council** (slice 5). Every card play the engine plans is "playing a card". A card goes to a distant Imperial site only as it could go to the pawn site, and a full Imperial site takes it only in place of one discarded there. The discard is optional, one card per play, stacks with the Homeland's, and holds at the pawn site only when it is Imperial. Nobody is asked to consent, and printed protections and locks still apply. An Imperial site is one the Empire's warbands hold when the play is planned. The holder's role does not matter. An unheld Grand Council has no previous holder, so all its favor goes to the banks. An exile-only game with an altered V starts the Grand Council in the shared bank.
14. **Festival** (slice 6). Every other player is the holder's enemy, whatever their role. "Their powers" are the powers of the denizens at the marked site, edifices included; relics and the site's own powers are untouched, and a denizen's action modifier is one of its powers. "Persistent" is the power as printed. Placing favor or secrets on a denizen is what City Wall's ban reads. Consent is asked outside a Campaign only. The marker starts beside the map, first goes on it at the holder's first Rest, and each Rest places it at the pawn's site, even where it already lies; a holder with no pawn on the map places nothing. With nobody holding the banner the marker guards nothing and stays where it lies. A take that walks no ribbon leaves the marker, which then guards for the new holder. "ONE" on an empty banner burns nothing. Under Secret Testament the holder takes every secret, and the marker still leaves the map. An exile-only game with an altered VI starts the Festival in the shared bank. An automatic power of a guarded card is refused for an enemy like any other non-persistent use, and Jinx at the festival is refused to an enemy who rules it. A Conspiracy that takes the Festival removes the marker, as a Raid does, since the right ribbon ends with the remove-festival icon (decided with the owner, 2026-11-12).
15. **Shape Foundation** (slice 7). "You may flip one foundation setup card from its altered side to its normal side unless it's being altered by an active legacy." An active legacy is one that a lineage this Chronicle records, seated or in the box, holds active; a source legacy that no lineage holds active does not stop the restore. 31. **Secret Society.** In a step after Populist and before Seer, the holder may take one Vision from the world deck, any but the Conspiracy, as a facedown adviser, and Visions Drawn advances once. The four Visions left are shuffled back into the card's layout, the denizens keeping their order: two at random among the first 12 cards and two among the next 17, or, with fewer than 25 denizens, anywhere in the deck. The Vision is not a starting adviser, so Oaths of Loyalty does not bury it; excess advisers are discarded right after the holder places their pawn (design, ruling 14).
32. **Seer.** In a step after Secret Society, with no "may", the holder sees every Vision in the world deck and gives each its final position, from the top, no two alike; the Visions are lifted out and inserted in ascending position, so each lands where chosen. Under Teeming World the holder sets the Visions' order (design, ruling 15).
33. **`ChooseAmounts` is answered as a distribution is,** one amount per slot, with no new answer form (R18).
34. **Secret Society's reshuffle is a journaled shuffle of its own pile,** `ShufflePile.VisionPackets` (R19).

36. **Secret Society's excess discard** comes after the holder's pawn and before their keep, one choose-many of exactly the excess with no Decline, the cards going where Setup's rejected advisers go, with a line naming them by the log's knowledge rule, since regional discard piles are hidden (R21).

38. **Seer's question** has one slot per Vision, 1 to the deck's size, distinct, no Decline, and is skipped only with no Vision or a deck of one card (R23).

40. **The ChooseAmounts control** has steppers and a number field on each row, and refuses two equal amounts (R25).

42. **The Standard Bearer.** In the holder's own pawn window, after the pawn's line, the holder may take a banner from the shared bank, the People's Favor or the Darkest Secret, and put two more of its own resource on it from the shared bank. No question when neither banner is there (design, ruling 16).
43. **Wealthy.** In the same window, with no "may": the holder takes 1 favor from each of two other players, or 3 favor from the shared bank. "Two players" is offered only when two other players hold favor, and with one option nothing is asked (design, ruling 17).
44. **Quest to Distant Lands, no pawn.** The holder's pawn question offers "Don't place your pawn". With no pawn the edifice powers of the pawn window do not act for them; The Standard Bearer and Wealthy still do. Their Choose Adviser, Secret Society discard and Reveal Cards wait for their first Wake (design, ruling 18).
45. **Quest to Distant Lands, swap.** A holder with no pawn may swap one card from the Dispossessed, which only they see, for one of their starting advisers or, with none, a card of the hand they drew. A swap is not a gain, so Oaths of Loyalty does not bury a card swapped in as a facedown adviser (design, ruling 19).
46. **Quest to Distant Lands, first Wake.** At the start of the Wake of a seated player with no pawn, before anything else, they must place it at any site in play; then the steps Setup deferred run. No pawn-window power fires. Until then the player stands at no site (design, ruling 20).
47. **The pawn window reads who placed from the latest pawn answer,** so a question asked in it keeps its shape; the edifice powers come first, then The Standard Bearer, then Wealthy (R27).
48. **The Standard Bearer's bank always gives two,** having no count (R28).
49. **Wealthy's questions** are a choice between two players and the bank, then a choose-many of exactly two players (R29), unless exactly two can give (item 54).
50. **Staying off writes "p2 placed no pawn with Quest to Distant Lands",** and the steps that read the pawn's region are skipped for any player with no pawn (R30).
51. **Quest's swap** peeks the Dispossessed for the holder, offers no Secret Society Vision, and comes before Choose Adviser would run (R31).
52. **The first Wake is a procedure of its own,** run before the Wake checks and Forced Wake, its questions `wake.legacy.quest.<player>` and its `.adviser`, `.discard` and `.reveal` sub-ids (R32).

54. **Wealthy with exactly two givers:** when exactly two other players hold favor, both give and no choose-many is asked (R34, amending R29).
55. **The first Wake's placement confirms as "Place your pawn at" and the site,** not Setup's "Start at" (R35).

59. **Iron Hand acts at setup only for a holder in the previous game:** both its warbands up to the Empire divider and every player's free pawn placement, in Setup With an Empire and With No Empire; a lineage that sat the previous game out is recorded with no previous player, and a TTS-imported Chronicle carries none, so Iron Hand does nothing after a TTS import (R39, owner re-ruling 2026-11-14).

62. **A game journaled before R39 and R40 may not replay:** an exile-only Chronicle with stored legacies, and a Chronicle whose Iron Hand holder's previous player reads differently, are refused or differ on replay; the alpha data policy makes no compatibility promise for them (R42).

## Legacy in-play powers rulings

Rulings for the powers legacies use during a game ([Legacy in-play powers design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-14-legacy-in-play-powers-design.md), and its [implementation rulings](../superpowers/specs/2026-11/2026-11-14-legacy-in-play-powers-implementation-rulings.md)). Items 1 to 13 are the design's rulings 1 to 13, taken with the owner on 2026-11-14; items 14 to 18 are implementation rulings R1 to R5; items 19 to 24 are the design's rulings 14 to 19; items 25 to 29 are implementation rulings R6 to R10; items 30 to 32 are the design's rulings 20 to 22; items 33 to 40 are implementation rulings R11 to R18, items 41 and 42 are R20 and R21 (R19 is a test's, not a rule's); items 43 to 47 are the design's rulings 23 to 27; items 48 to 56 are implementation rulings R22 to R30.

1. **Only seated players' active legacies act.** Dormant legacies, a boxed lineage's legacies and the Public Ambitions row never act; The Mouth is the one way a legacy becomes active during a game (design, ruling 1).
2. **"If you are an Imperial" is read when the power is used,** so the Crown and Citizenship change it during a game (design, ruling 2).
3. **A legacy ACTION is a minor action** in the Act menu, with the legacy named as its source, under the same reuse and cost rules as any other ACTION power; its burnt cost is paid as a card's is (design, ruling 3).
4. **Passive powers check their holder; chosen powers carry a source** (design, ruling 4).
5. **A legacy is in its holder's play area,** so rules that read a play area for powers, such as Imperial Tactics, reach it (design, ruling 5).
6. **The seam.** A legacy is a power source wherever a menu, a cost or a decision resolves one. Ruthless, Golden Key and The Mouth print their burnt costs (design, ruling 6).
7. **Ruthless.** The holder buries any card in their site's denizen row, locked or not, edifices included; its tokens return as a bury returns them. It is not offered when the site has none (design, ruling 7).
8. **Peacemaker.** Offered while the holder holds a banner; holding both, they choose which. The banner goes to the shared bank with its tokens on it, then every site and every player board keeps one warband of what it holds, the holder's included: removed warbands return to their bank, bandits to the bandit supply (design, ruling 8).
9. **The Reliquary is always in play.** A non-empty Reliquary is shown in the shared bank, facedown; its faces only to a viewer allowed to see them (design, ruling 9).
10. **Golden Key.** An Imperial holder may always peek at the Reliquary; its ACTION takes any relic from it, facedown, as the Chancellor takes one at Setup. It is not a Recover, and is not offered while the Reliquary is empty (design, ruling 10).
11. **High Priest's ACTION.** Offered while the holder holds the Darkest Secret with a secret on it; they move 1 to all of its secrets to their board (design, ruling 11).
12. **The Ear's ACTION.** Free: the holder picks a site with a relic they have not identified and peeks at all its relics, knowing them while they stay there (design, ruling 12).
13. **Each power writes one line naming the legacy:** "p1 buried Herbalist with Ruthless", "p2 returned the People's Favor with Peacemaker", "p3 took a relic from the Reliquary with Golden Key"; The Ear's is public and never names the relics (design, ruling 13).
14. **Golden Key's Imperial condition gates both halves:** an Exile holder neither peeks nor is offered the ACTION (R1).
15. **The Ear's "not identified" is recorded knowledge:** a relic its holder has not peeked counts, at their own pawn's site too (R2).
16. **High Priest's and The Ear's lines** are "p2 moved 3 secrets from the Darkest Secret with High Priest" and "p2 peeked at the relics at Deep Woods with The Ear" (R3).
17. **An ACTION with nothing to do is shown unavailable with its reason,** as card ACTIONs are; only Golden Key for an Exile holder is left out of the menu (R4).
18. **A set-aside Reliquary shows nothing and gives nothing:** the shared bank and Golden Key read the Reliquary in play, so the design's ruling 9 does not override [Foundations ruling 3](#foundations-rulings) (R5).

19. **Ancestral Lands.** An Imperial holder's travel to a Cradle site costs 1 Supply, applied after every other travel-cost change unless one has already made it lower; only the cost changes, so a Pass still restricts (design, ruling 14).
20. **Pathfinder.** The holder's travel ignores the Mountain and Island cost powers and the Pass's travel restriction, always and without a question; the Pass's campaign-targeting limit still applies (design, ruling 15).
21. **Keeper of Order.** A battle plan with the legacy as its source, usable once per campaign by the attacker or the defender while the enemy is an Imperial player: its user adds or removes 2 attack dice, and an attacker ignores every skull they roll. Another Imperial may use it through Imperial Tactics with the holder's consent (design, ruling 16).
22. **Light Fingers.** Take Wealth's "no enemy pawns" gate is a restriction, which Light Fingers' holder ignores, always and without a question (design, ruling 17).
23. **Beloved.** When the holder trades, the sources widen to the faceup, empty advisers of other players whose pawns are at their site, with no consent; Trade's own rules are unchanged, and a facedown adviser is excluded (design, ruling 18).
24. **High Priest, when negotiating.** The holder may give secrets in a deal, one way; a start-fact marker keeps the term on resume (design, ruling 19).
25. **Pathfinder ignores Lost Lagoon,** the Island power at its site (R6).
26. **Keeper of Order's sign is a follow-up question,** "Add 2 attack dice" or "Remove 2 attack dice", as Mountain Giant asks (R7).
27. **The passive modifiers write no lines;** Keeper of Order writes "Added 2 attack dice." or "Removed 2 attack dice.", and "Skulls ignored." when it ignores a skull; High Priest's secrets show in the deal's own lines (R8).
28. **A Negotiation saved before High Priest's list** resumes with it empty (R9).
29. **High Priest left the unimplemented list with its ACTION,** before its WHEN NEGOTIATING half landed (R10).
30. **The Needle.** After a banner moves into the holder's play area by a move the walk executes (a challenge, a raid, Favored Son, Wizard's Conclave, the Conspiracy, The Standard Bearer at Setup), the holder chooses "Keep this side" or "Flip it"; a banner placed at game start is not taken. Flipping keeps the banner's tokens; flipping to the Festival places no marker, and flipping away from it removes the marker (design, ruling 20).
31. **Arbiter.** Just before the end die is rolled for Stable Regime, at the end of rounds 5 to 7, the holder chooses Decline, −1, +1, +2 or +3, publicly. The roll's total is the face plus the modifier, read against the printed rolls as "at least": 6 or more after round 5, 5 or more after round 6, 3 or more after round 7 (design, ruling 21).
32. **Iron Hand during a game.** When its holder becomes Imperial, by any path to Citizenship, Iron Hand goes to the legacy deck right after the role changes, as the Chronicle's discard sends it (design, ruling 22).
33. **Arbiter's choice waits in the game state** until the roll reads and clears it; a round that ends without a roll clears it too, so no modifier reaches a later round (R11).
34. **The slice's lines.** "p2 flipped the People's Favor with The Needle", and nothing for keeping the side; "p2 modified the end die by +2 with Arbiter", then "The end die rolled 4, modified by +2 to 6. Stable Regime needed 6.", with the old line for an unmodified roll and nothing for Decline; "p3 discarded Iron Hand on becoming a Citizen", or "… on becoming the Chancellor" in a game with no Empire (R12).
35. **The plainest answer is asked first:** The Needle's "Keep this side", then "Flip it"; Arbiter's Decline, then −1, +1, +2, +3 (R13).
36. **One batch handing The Needle's holder both banners asks once per banner,** the People's Favor first (R14).
37. **Pathfinder ignores only Lost Lagoon's Island add** on arriving at its site, never its Coast power on leaving it, which narrows item 25 (R15).
38. **Take Wealth's pawn gate may refuse after the resource check:** with an enemy pawn there and the resource missing, the refusal names the missing resource; the menu leaves the option out either way (R16).
39. **Old Songs stacks with Beloved:** a Beloved source with exactly one secret and no favor qualifies (R17).
40. **Imperial Infighting does not hide the enemy Imperial's lendable plans:** CR p. 35 makes the enemy a lawful lender, and the plan's own limit keeps the attacker from using it (R18).
41. **Every banner taker keeps its question and its line** when a walk parks right after the take for The Needle's question and resumes: the question comes inside the taking walk, right after the take, as the design's ruling 20 times it (R20).
42. **Beloved's token on another player's adviser leaves at the next Rest,** as every card's tokens do (R21).
43. **The Mouth's targets.** The holder's own dormant legacies, and the Public Ambitions legacies, whose goals the holder meets when the ACTION is used, read as Resolve Legacies reads them; another player's dormant legacy is no target (design, ruling 23).
44. **The Mouth's activation.** An own legacy flips faceup, which reveals it; a public one moves faceup into the holder's lineage. A legacy that alters a foundation alters it now; with more than three active legacies the holder discards to three, flipping no foundation back. The activated legacy is in effect at once (design, ruling 24).
45. **Conspirator's prediction** is a forced Wake power in round 1: the holder writes a round from 1 to 8 and a winner among the seated players, themselves included. A Conspirator gained later never writes; a Quest to Distant Lands holder places their pawn first (design, ruling 25).
46. **The prediction is hidden** from every other viewer until the game ends; the holder sees it (design, ruling 26).
47. **At the game's end** the round it ended in and the final winner, after any Successor, are compared with the prediction; if both match and the holder still holds Conspirator active, the holder wins instead. The prediction is revealed either way, and a Conspirator lost during the game voids it (design, ruling 27).
48. **A flip's line comes before the taker's line,** as The Needle's question parks right after the take (R22).
49. **Iron Hand's in-game line** is a trigger line before the action's own line (R23).
50. **The prediction is compared at the game's end,** before the Chronicle and so before Royal Line (R24).
51. **The Mouth may activate Iron Hand for an Imperial holder;** the same batch then discards it, with Iron Hand's line (R25).
52. **The holder sees the prediction** in a log line only they read (R26).
53. **The slice's lines:** "p2 wrote down a prediction with Conspirator" for everyone; "p2 predicted p3 wins in round 6" for the holder; "p2 had written down p3 to win in round 6 with Conspirator" at the end; "p2 won with Conspirator". The Mouth: "p1 activated Arbiter with The Mouth", "p1 took Golden Key from the Public Ambitions with The Mouth", "Beloved set Foundation IV to Teeming World", "p1 discarded Seer with The Mouth" (R27).
54. **The round is an amount** no log line names, and no "chose" line names the winner (R28).
55. **A void prediction** is revealed with the same line (R29).
56. **The Mouth with nothing to activate** is shown unavailable with "No legacy whose goal you meet" (R30).
